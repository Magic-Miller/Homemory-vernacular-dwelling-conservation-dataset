# Expert Review Guide

## Purpose

Professional review improves annotation reliability and may add conservation knowledge without presenting image-only interpretation as definitive diagnosis or repair design.

## Reviewer Information

The project should record the reviewer's relevant role or area of experience, review date, and declared conflicts of interest. Public attribution must follow the reviewer's consent.

## Annotation Review

For each reviewed annotation, record one of the following outcomes:

- accepted;
- accepted with revision;
- disputed;
- not assessable from the available evidence;
- rejected.

Revisions must preserve the original annotation and provenance.

## Adding Conservation Knowledge

Where evidence permits, reviewers may add:

- one or more possible causes;
- alternative interpretations;
- risk indicators and escalation notes;
- recommended next inspection steps;
- general conservation principles;
- discouraged or potentially harmful interventions;
- maintenance considerations;
- supporting references or comparable cases.

## Required Caution

- Distinguish observed facts from interpretation.
- State uncertainty and missing evidence.
- Avoid site-specific repair prescriptions based only on photographs.
- Do not assign structural-safety conclusions without an appropriate assessment.
- Recognize that local materials, climate, craft practice, and regulatory context may change the interpretation.

## Disagreement

Legitimate expert disagreement should be recorded through parallel interpretations or a review discussion. Consensus must not be manufactured by deleting minority views without an audit trail.

## Release Recommendation

The reviewer may recommend publication, further documentation, additional specialist review, restricted access, or exclusion. Final release decisions also depend on consent, privacy, quality, and licensing checks.
